# Methods summary for SODA 308

This project studies Singapore's development across economic performance, living standards, happiness, freedom, and social outcomes. Singapore is compared with ten selected countries or economies and a larger international reference group. The selected cases do not determine the international average.

The project uses R and real data from the World Bank, V-Dem, and the World Happiness Report. Original files, source versions, definitions, and country matches are saved. Hong Kong is treated as a separately reported economy. Missing values remain missing.

For annual indicators, z-scores compare an economy with the available international reference in the same year. At least 100 economies are required. Indicators where lower values are better are reversed. Income is logged. Provisional category scores summarize economic performance, living standards, and freedom. Happiness uses a published life-evaluation score covering several years, so it is kept separate from annual observations. Social measures remain separate until their survey definitions are reviewed.

The expanded models study economic growth, living standards, and life evaluation. They examine democracy, openness, government effectiveness, and selected controls. Annual predictors come from the previous year. Happiness predictors come from before each non-overlapping reporting period. A separate freedom model omits democracy because those measures share concepts and components.

The models compare country and year effects, income controls, alternative openness measures, and selected samples. They report uncertainty and sample sizes. State intervention still needs a defensible measure, so the full four-variable model is not claimed. Statistical associations cannot prove that institutions caused development or that Singapore's policies will work elsewhere.
